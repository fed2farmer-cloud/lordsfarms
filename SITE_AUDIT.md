# LordsFarms.store — Public Site Audit

## Strong parts worth keeping
- Clear homepage value proposition.
- Separate product, experience, story, veteran-program and contact sections.
- Direct calls to action for retail, experiences, wholesale and partnerships.
- Simple category-based store structure.

## Problems found in the live site
1. Placeholder contact information is public: `(555) 123-4567` and `123 Farm Road` appear on the site.
2. Public test inventory exists: `Test Product` and `TEST_unauth` are visible in the fruit catalog.
3. The site repeatedly uses `Certified Organic` / `organic` language. That should be confirmed against the farm's current certification status before publication.
4. The Homegrown By Heroes page states verified certification. Current authorization should be verified before the official badge or certified language is used.
5. Product sourcing is unclear. Seafood such as Chilean sea bass, salmon and oysters appears next to farm products without clearly saying whether it is farm-raised or third-party sourced.
6. Some catalog items do not naturally match an Antelope Valley outdoor farm (for example soursop and jackfruit) unless greenhouse/controlled-environment production is actually part of the operation.
7. Health-oriented product copy such as “blood-sugar support” is unnecessary risk for a food storefront and should be replaced with flavor, culinary and farm-origin descriptions.
8. Hot-air-balloon experiences are presented as directly bookable. This should be framed as partner-operated/planned until a licensed/insured operator and booking workflow are in place.
9. The site mixes current operations and long-term vision without clear status labels. Customers cannot tell what exists today versus what is planned.
10. The footer branding alternates between “Lord's Farm” and “Lords Farms LLC.” The rebuild standardizes the public brand as “Lords Farms.”

## Rebuild strategy
- Keep the best commercial structure.
- Make every product and attraction status explicit.
- Centralize editable facts and catalog data.
- Use the current farm vision as the brand differentiator.
- Avoid claims that could create regulatory or trust problems until verified.
